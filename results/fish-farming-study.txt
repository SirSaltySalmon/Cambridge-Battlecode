FISH vs v170 FARMING STUDY (2026-11-03)

Data: 25 Intercontinental Fish ladder games (16-9 against Mite x10, test4 x5, Univardisty of Shingtonwash x5,
NEETCoders x5) and v170 in 22 local mirror games on the same 22 maps (44 team-games). Every game was replayed
through the visualiser's engine for per-round positions, health, gold, gems and camp states; v170's debug decision
logs show why its champions did what they did.
Caveat: Fish played top teams on the ladder, v170 played itself locally. Farming-time numbers compare well;
fighting numbers (deaths, kills) do not.


THE SHORT VERSION

Fish does not kill camps faster: both teams get the same gems per farming round (0.81 vs 0.82 captured gems per
champion-round spent hitting or capturing). Fish spends more of its time farming and turns gold into items sooner.
Camp damage per alive champion-round is about 40% higher for Fish before round 600.

WHAT FISH DOES DIFFERENTLY

1. Split farming
   Fish opens together on one good group, then splits after ~3 captures (median r66, with 0 emblems). The usual
   shape is a core of 3 plus 1-2 solo or duo farmers on own-side and middle wolves and elks, ~17 tiles apart and
   often on the other flank. It regroups for elk packs, golems, monoliths and fights, then splits again. Squad size
   is "the fewest champions whose expected camp damage stays under ~0.3-0.5 of their health". It never sends a solo
   or duo to the enemy side.
   v170 has one team target, chosen by a leader each round, and never splits on normal maps (its split code only
   runs for Cassi & Issac, whom v170 never drafts). It brings 1.3-1.7 more champions to a camp than it needs.

2. Respawns and waiting
   Fish almost never waits: while a camp respawns it farms something else and comes back in time. Its waits are a
   few rounds long, and alone.
   When v170's team target is respawning and no filler beats it, the whole team walks to it and waits 2 tiles off,
   with a median 22 rounds left on the timer and a live own or middle camp within 10 steps half the time.

3. Which camps
   Per decision both teams mostly go to the nearest group where they are the team's closest champion ("nearest"
   alone predicts ~47% of Fish's choices and ~48% of v170's). The differences:
   - Fish weighs value early (about +20 walking steps per 100 gold and -27 per 100 camp strength before r150) and
     fades to nearest-first by about r500. It takes gold-only groups early (2.2 a game before r150 vs 0.9).
   - Fish avoids groups an ally is already on (it spreads out); v170 drifts to its allies (one shared target).
   - Opening: ~4 Fish champions take the best group near spawn (an elk group in 48% of games) and capture it around
     r26 for ~15 gems. v170's first capture is at r27 for 7 gems, often a cluster.
   - Middle: Fish races only for valuable middle groups (elk, golem, monolith), timed to their respawn, and leaves
     low-value middle clusters and wolves to the enemy.
   - Both skip groups the enemy has already damaged.

4. When a camp is allowed (thresholds)
   Fish goes by damage and health, not item counts. Its implicit rule: send the smallest squad whose expected camp
   damage taken is at most about half its health. Elk packs with 3+ champions from the first rounds; solo wolves from
   ~r240 and solo elks from ~r330 (when they happen); golems from ~r110 with 4-6 bare champions; monoliths at ~6
   emblems per champion with 4 present, or chipped by one champion over several visits. Camps never heal, so Fish
   hits a strong camp until its weakest member is near 50% health, heals at spawn, and comes back.
   v170 counts items: an elk group needs (elks - champions present) <= emblems of those present; a golem 4-10
   emblems among those present; a monolith 15/18/20 team emblems with everyone grouped at 80% health. Its first
   golem engagement is at r523 (Fish r108) and its first monolith around r1300.

5. Side of the map
   Fish: 57% of captures on its own side, 41% middle, 3% enemy side, but 63% of its gems come from the middle
   (bigger groups). It commits to one flank early, usually the one away from the enemy's opening.
   v170: zones by walking-step ratio (safe / uncertain / unsafe at 1.5x); the middle is open unless 2+ champions
   down; the enemy side only in raids; a contest bonus of up to 2x pulls the team to middle groups.

6. Items and gold
   Fish shops on income (recalls with ~90-190 gold at ~50% health, 2.6x as often as v170). Its first emblems are
   farming emblems: Capture (3-round captures on 44% of its captures in r150-300 vs 4% for v170), Prospector, then
   Jungle and Monster. Gold is spread over 2-3 frontline farmers.
   v170 shops after a tribute with an empty bag (150 gold); Vesper batches 450 gold after her first emblem and takes
   56% of the team's gold. Its first emblems are Treasure (Vesper) and Capture only on Heart of Ruin.

7. Draft
   Fish always takes a carry + Vendetta + Heart of Ruin + Cassi & Issac (+ Willow, Varg or Ren). Cassi & Issac is
   one pick but two bodies, so Fish fields 6 champions on 5 picks (23 of 25 games). Carry: Vesper when the spawns
   are far apart by walking (more than ~62 steps), Marcella when close (fits 17 of 19 maps; possibly over-fitted).
   v170 drafts Ryth, Heart of Ruin, Willow, Vesper, Marcella (the Prism team on gem-cluster maps) and never Cassi &
   Issac; two of its picks go to Ryth and Willow, the weakest farmers in either dataset.

8. Map size
   Fish's lead does not depend on map size or spawn distance (ahead on captures by r300 on 19 of 22 maps). What
   scales is splitting: Fish's parallel farming grows with the number of own/middle non-cluster camps per champion
   (rank correlation 0.72). v170's fixed tile constants (8-step loot, 60-tile plan radius) do not scale.

9. Pathing
   Both spend the same share of peaceful time walking to camps (47-48%). Fish buys Teleport Crystals on maps where
   one 3-tile jump saves 30+ walking steps (20-36 teleports a game there); v170 never does.

10. Captures
   v170 leaves cleared clusters uncaptured a mean 20.5 rounds (Fish 7), and a cleared group does not start its
   respawn until captured. Causes: passing "free hits" with nobody to collect, and the single team claim.

Side finding: pre-armed recalls. Fish, Mite, test4 and Univardisty start a recall at the end of nearly every turn
and cancel it at their next turn start (87-92% of their off-spawn rows; NEETCoders 1%, v170 0%), so between their
turns they read as recalling. v170's fight score gave recalling enemies weight 0, and its target pick chased them.

Splitting lost because v170's fights punish spread champions: v170's raids hit the split side when it was down
in bodies (outnumbered 51% of rounds vs 17%). Fish's splitting works inside Fish's whole system; ours would need
regroup and fight rules built for it first.

Not tested yet: Fish's draft (Cassi & Issac + Vendetta + Heart of Ruin); pre-arming recalls ourselves (a mimic that
only pre-armed was even with v170, +0.3%); teleport crystals; fixing the respawn waiting without splitting.
Local results overstate ladder results, so bots/v202 needs top-50 ladder games before it replaces anything.
